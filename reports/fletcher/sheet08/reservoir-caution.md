# Modern reservoir context for physical matching

The native scan shows Gisborne Lake and Wreck Cove Lakes with substantially different outlines from the modern NSTDB water polygons. These outlines are not treated as interchangeable shore controls.

Nova Scotia Power dates construction of the Wreck Cove system to 1975–1978, well after this Fletcher survey. Its published hydro asset study identifies Gisborne and Wreck Cove reservoirs, plus Ingonish I/II and McMillan reservoirs. This supports checking for altered water geometry in those areas rather than interpreting every mismatch as an alignment error.

Primary sources consulted 2026-09-11:

- [Nova Scotia Power — Wreck Cove](https://www.nspower.ca/cleanandgreen/clean-energy/clean-energy-sources/wreck-cove)
- [Nova Scotia Power — 2018 Hydro Asset Study, Appendix B reservoir inventory](https://www.nspower.ca/docs/default-source/irp/20181221-ns-power-hydro-asset-study-redacted.pdf?sfvrsn=a8dabcf6_1)
- [Canadian Geographical Names Database — Gisborne Flowage, formerly Gisborne Lake](https://geonames.nrcan.gc.ca/recherche-de-noms-de-lieux/unique?id=b4d8c058affd11d892e2080020a0f4c9&wbdisable=true)

This is context for rejecting unstable correspondences. It does not establish a precise historical shoreline, elevation or dam footprint, nor validate any control by name alone.
