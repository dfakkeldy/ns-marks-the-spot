# Judique pilot: historical-record follow-up

Reviewed September 6, 2026. These findings supplement the candidate-area review; they do not change source anchors, the frozen warp or historical-site geometry.

## St. Andrew's church — 015

Janette MacDonald's *The Historical and Social Development of Judique*, submitted to St. Francis Xavier University in 1968, explicitly says the stone church stands where its predecessor stood. The digitized religious-history excerpt is [preserved by Library and Archives Canada](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/205/301/ic/cdc/celtic/judrel.htm?nodisclaimer=1). It records the wooden church's destruction by lightning in 1919, the replacement cornerstone on Dominion Day 1924, and consecration in August 1927. This supplies historical support for reuse of the site, beyond parish identity and geographic resemblance.

[The Casket, August 27, 1925, page 5](https://antigonishheritage.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/08-August-27-1925.pdf#page=5), reprints a Glace Bay Gazette account of construction under Father Ronald McDonald. It describes Gothic design, hand-cut native stone and a planned copper roof. This contemporary report corroborates construction, but does not locate the former foundations.

There is a chronology conflict: MacDonald says the larger wooden church was begun in 1851 and completed in 1879; the [Participaper's 2025 retrospective](https://participaperonline.ca/judique-festival-marks-250-years-since-founding/) gives 1860 and describes a move farther from the shore with a new cemetery. Do not silently choose one completion date. A pre-1919 photograph, plan or original parish construction record could resolve both chronology and footprint.

## Southern mills — 077, 078 (B), 079

The contemporary article **“Lights and Shadows of Early Settlement in Judique”**, [The Antigonish Casket, September 1, 1892, page 1](https://antigonishheritage.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/09-September-01-1892.pdf#page=1), describes a glen behind Long Point and names mills belonging to **Alex. Chisholm and Donald Chisholm** on its river. The coordinator checked the masthead and relevant third-column passage in the scan; the local PDF archive and SHA-256 are recorded with annotation 078's external source.

This provides two names for further investigation. It does not distinguish the three Fletcher annotations, identify a bank or waterworks, or specify mill types. The article's historical river description also needs to be matched independently to the modern catchment. Neither name is assigned to Mill B or promoted to a property claim.

Next: seek mill descriptions, business-directory entries or historical land documents for those names at Long Point, looking for spatial details that distinguish the separate crossings and buildings. User residence information and private location observations are excluded from this public note.
