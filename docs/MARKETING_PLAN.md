## Positioning

NS Marks The Spot is a field companion for comparing Nova Scotia's Fletcher
historical maps with today's landscape. The marketing should make the core
promise immediate: old maps over modern context, adjustable transparency,
Nova Scotia reference layers, and clear source/licensing disclosures.

Do not position it as a legal property, surveying, navigation, emergency, flood
risk, or title-search tool. The strongest trust signal is that the app is
specific about what each layer can and cannot be used for.

## Strategic Goals

1. Finish a review-safe launch package: screenshots, support URL, privacy URL,
   category, pricing, age rating, export compliance, and final App Store copy.
2. Convert interested Nova Scotians with one clear default product page instead
   of splitting traffic too early.
3. Recruit a small, high-signal beta group that can validate field behavior,
   disclaimers, offline Fletcher areas, and data-source expectations.
4. Build a public trail through the website/devlog so the project feels active,
   transparent, and locally grounded.
5. Capture post-launch evidence before scaling into paid acquisition or App
   Store experiments.

## Message Pillars

### Compare Then And Now

Lead with the overlay interaction: slide Fletcher historical maps over modern
basemaps to inspect old roads, shorelines, place names, and land features.

### Built For Nova Scotia Field Research

Keep the audience local and specific. This is more compelling than a generic
historical-map viewer because the app carries Nova Scotia layers, caveats, and
offline Fletcher preparation.

### Useful Context, Not Legal Advice

Reference layers such as property boundaries, Crown lands, flood-risk areas,
and waterfalls are context layers. Listing copy, screenshots, and outreach
should repeat that they are reference-only and not navigation-grade.

### Transparent Sources

Use Data Sources & Licenses as a marketing asset. The app can earn trust by
showing where the maps and services come from and what limitations apply.

## Competitive Context

Kickstart does not currently track dedicated NS Marks competitors. A July 1,
2026 App Store search scan showed adjacent apps in four groups:

| Adjacent app type | Examples | What they own | NS Marks angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broad historical-map tools | OldMapsOnline | Large historical-map discovery | More focused Nova Scotia field workflow and reference layers |
| Offline map tools | Avenza Maps, Nova Scotia Offline Map, Topo Maps+ | Offline and GPS-first map use | Historical Fletcher overlays and source-specific caveats |
| Parcel/property tools | LandGlide, Land id, Regrid | Parcel and ownership-oriented use | Avoid legal/ownership claims; offer local context instead |
| Outdoor/hunting maps | onX Hunt, BaseMap | Recreation, land access, GPS | Nova Scotia history and place comparison, not outdoor navigation |

The listing should not try to out-feature large GIS or outdoor-map products.
The differentiation is specificity, trust, and the pleasure of comparing old
and current geography.

## Recommended Features - Eligible Now

### High Priority

#### App Store Assets

- Why: screenshots are the main conversion tool before there is traffic data.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: strongest immediate lift for launch confidence.
- Action: capture current iPhone and iPad screenshots from the actual build.

Recommended screenshot sequence:

1. Fletcher overlay over modern Nova Scotia.
2. Transparency comparison with old and current features visible.
3. Nova Scotia reference layers with clear "reference only" framing.
4. Waterfall POIs or a field-research example.
5. Prepared Fletcher offline area.
6. Data Sources & Licenses trust screen.

#### Default Product Page

- Why: the app is too early for product-page experiments, so the default page
  needs to be strong before launch.
- Effort: quick win.
- Impact: high for first impressions.
- Action: keep the current focused subtitle, review keywords after screenshots
  exist, and avoid claims that imply legal parcel accuracy or navigation use.

#### Website And Devlog

- Why: the GitHub Pages site can explain the project more freely than the App
  Store listing and gives reviewers, testers, and local groups a stable link.
- Effort: quick win now that the site exists.
- Impact: medium, compounding.
- Action: keep the devlog current and add launch notes when TestFlight feedback
  or App Store submission milestones are real.

#### Beta Outreach

- Why: a small local beta is more useful than broad low-context acquisition.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: high for launch quality.
- Action: invite testers from Nova Scotia history, genealogy, hiking, waterfall,
  archive, and mapping circles. Ask them to test the exact flows in the
  TestFlight "What to Test" file.

### Medium Priority

#### Featuring Nomination

- Why: a polished, local, historically grounded map app has a clearer editorial
  story than a generic utility.
- Effort: quick win once assets and review readiness are complete.
- Impact: high if selected, uncertain otherwise.
- Action: prepare a nomination around local history, transparent data sources,
  iPad map exploration, and responsible location/privacy handling.

#### Community And Press Outreach

- Why: the app's audience is concentrated in local communities that can provide
  qualified installs and feedback.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: medium before launch, higher after public availability.
- Action: create a short launch note for local history societies, archive
  groups, genealogy forums, Nova Scotia hiking/waterfall communities, and
  regional newsletters.

#### App Preview Video

- Why: the overlay slider is visual and benefits from motion.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: medium.
- Action: record a 15- to 30-second preview after the screenshot set is stable.

### Nice To Have

#### In-App Event

- Why: App Store events can help discoverability even for small apps, but only
  when there is a real public event or content story.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: medium after launch.
- Action: revisit for a launch event, a Fletcher-map exploration week, or a
  local-history seasonal story after the app is live.

#### Apple Search Ads

- Why: a small exact-match campaign can validate search terms once conversion
  tracking exists.
- Effort: moderate.
- Impact: unknown until screenshots and listing are live.
- Action: wait until the public listing has baseline conversion data, then test
  narrow terms such as "Nova Scotia maps", "old maps", "property map", and
  "genealogy maps" with strict caps.

#### Pre-Orders

- Why: useful only if there is a fixed launch date and an audience to announce
  it to.
- Effort: low.
- Impact: low to medium.
- Action: skip unless the App Store timeline becomes calendar-driven.

## App Store Listing Guidance

Keep:

- Name: `NS Marks the Spot`
- Subtitle direction: `Historical Nova Scotia maps`
- Core promise: compare Fletcher historical maps with modern Nova Scotia.
- Privacy line: location is optional and only shows current position.

Tighten before submission:

- Category, pricing, territories, age rating, and support/privacy URLs.
- Screenshot captions that use cautious language for reference layers.
- Keywords after reviewing screenshot copy and App Store Connect category.
- Review notes that explain optional location, historical-map limitations, and
  restricted-service attribution.

Avoid:

- "Accurate property boundaries"
- "Navigation"
- "Survey-grade"
- "Flood-risk assessment"
- "Offline Nova Scotia maps" without specifying Fletcher-only saved areas.

2. Capture the screenshot set from the current build.
3. Review and finalize metadata against the screenshots.
4. Confirm the privacy manifest and App Review notes are aligned with claims.
5. Invite a focused TestFlight group and collect feedback.
6. Publish a short launch/devlog update from real milestones.
7. Submit for App Review.
8. Monitor search impressions, product-page conversion, crashes, feedback, and
   support questions.
9. Prepare a featuring nomination after the public listing and launch assets are
   stable.
10. Revisit PPO, custom product pages, and paid acquisition only after traffic
    volume justifies them.
